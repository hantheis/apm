# Output quality

_Portable core. Version 2, 13 September 2026. A standing rule-set, declared once at operator level and inherited into every brief through the context pack, alongside the house style. The house style says how an output reads and what it cites; this says whether it is finished. Executing agents tick the hard rule in the pre-delivery self-check; soft rules are defaults a brief may override._

## Hard rules

1. Nothing left for the reviewer that the brief let the agent do. No placeholders, no "to be confirmed" the inputs could confirm, no option list where the brief asked for a recommendation, no step deferred to the reader that was inside the brief and the budget.

## Soft rules

- Verified, not plausible. Every finding was checked against a named input, not inferred from what seems likely. (The house style's hard rules 2 and 3 carry the tickable half of this: cite a named input; state what could not be verified.)
- The reader's next action is clear near the top: what to decide, do, or check, or that nothing is needed. For a document the delivery summary's "what to check" line carries this; for a comment the comment itself should.
- Reason from the actual problem, not the usual shape. Before following a template or a convention, check that it fits this task's inputs; say when it does not.
- Prefer the choice with the higher expected value for the reader, and say why in one line, over the choice that is merely safe or customary.
- Use every lever the brief and rule-sets offer: the named inputs in full, related prior tickets, the resolved template, the context pack's glossary and stakeholders. An unused lever the brief named is a gap to explain.
- No filler: no restating the request, no preamble about what the output is about to do, no hedged conclusion where the inputs support a plain one.
- At equal content, shorter wins.

## How to cite this rule-set

In a context pack or a brief: `output quality (rules/output-quality.md)`. The delivery summary names it under "Rules applied"; the self-check ticks its one hard rule.
